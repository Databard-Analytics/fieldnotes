# Fieldnotes Writing Rules

## Core tone

Fieldnotes writing should be:

- calm
- intelligent
- practical
- reflective
- clear
- commercially relevant
- never hypey
- never academic for its own sake

## Voice principles

### 1. Write like a strategic practitioner, not a content marketer

Prefer:

- judgment over performance
- clarity over cleverness
- substance over trend-chasing

### 2. Stay close to real decisions

Every brief should feel connected to:

- leadership choices
- business tradeoffs
- capability building
- human behavior
- practical consequences

### 3. Be thoughtful, but not vague

Observations should land clearly.
Avoid abstract word clouds.
Always name the implication.

### 4. Sound informed, not inflated

Avoid exaggerated certainty, empty futurism, and grand language that is not earned.
Use words like "revolutionary", "game-changing", or "paradigm shift" only when they are genuinely justified.

### 5. Keep the writing human

The voice should be:

- warm but not chatty
- authoritative but not stiff
- reflective but not indulgent

## Style rules

### Prefer

- short to medium sentences
- crisp openings
- specific nouns
- concrete implications
- grounded language
- elegant simplicity

### Avoid

- jargon stacks
- long corporate phrasing
- generic motivational language
- forced thought-leadership tone
- overexplaining obvious things

## What a daily brief should do

A brief should do at least one of these:

- clarify a trend
- reframe a problem
- surface a strategic implication
- connect two ideas in a useful way
- challenge shallow consensus
- make complexity easier to act on

## What a daily brief should not do

A brief should not:

- just summarize news
- repeat obvious AI commentary
- posture as certainty when it is really speculation
- wander without a point
- sound like LinkedIn bait

## Recommended brief structure

A daily brief should usually include:

1. **Headline**  
   Clear, specific, and interesting.

2. **Opening observation**  
   What changed, what matters, or what is being noticed.

3. **Interpretation**  
   Why it matters strategically.

4. **Implication**  
   What leaders, founders, or professionals should pay attention to.

5. **Closing line**  
   A crisp takeaway, question, or framing thought.

## Editorial checks before publishing

Before publishing, ask:

- Is there a real observation here?
- Does this help someone think better?
- Is the implication clear?
- Does it sound like lived judgment rather than recycled commentary?
- Is it aligned with Databard’s voice of clarity, signal, and practical leadership?

## Tone reference

**Thoughtful, practical, strategically literate writing for people making real decisions in an AI-shaped world.**
